Summer Retention Offer — Campaign Performance Summary
Objective: Evaluate whether the Summer Retention Offer generated sufficient incremental revenue and return on marketing investment to justify broader deployment.

Executive Decision
Scale selectively, don't scale broadly yet. The campaign is profitable in aggregate (55% ROMI) and every segment shows positive incremental revenue — but performance is heavily concentrated in two segments (Champion, Growth) while the other two (Win-Back, Churn Watchlist) are marginal. Recommend scaling investment in the strong segments now, while running a larger-sample test on the weaker segments before committing further budget — the control groups are too small (15–39 customers) to be confident those numbers will hold up at scale.

Results
Test outperformed control across all four segments on both response and conversion:
Metric				Test				Control				Lift		
Response rate		23.6% (113/479)		21.7%(26/120)		+1.9 pts
Conversion rate		16.3% (78/479)		9.2% (11/120)		+7.1 pts (~77% relative)

Conversion lift is the more meaningful signal here — response rates were close between groups, but test-group responders converted to buyers at a much higher rate than control-group responders.

Segment Findings

Champion drove the campaign. It's less than 15% of total contacts (73 of 599) but generated 43% of incremental revenue, at a segment ROMI nearly 8x the overall average. 
Win-Back diluted efficiency — smallest conversion lift (1 point) and lowest segment ROMI, despite similar cost allocation to other segments. 
Growth is the best "scale" candidate: strong lift and a large enough segment (196 contacts) to matter at volume.

Financial Impact
The campaign generated an estimated $276 in incremental revenue against $178 in campaign costs, resulting in $98 of net incremental revenue and a 55% ROMI. 
All four segments exceeded break-even, although profitability varied considerably by segment.


Recommendations
Scale Champion and Growth segments — clearest lift, strongest ROMI, and Growth has the contact volume to make a scaled rollout worthwhile.
Hold Win-Back and Churn Watchlist at current investment — don't cut them (they're profitable), but don't expand spend until a larger test confirms the lift is real and not sample noise.


Limitations
Sample size: Control groups range from just 15 to 39 customers per segment — far too small for statistical confidence. Treat all lift figures as directional, not statistically validated.
Short-term revenue only: ROMI is based on immediate campaign revenue, not margin, and doesn't capture longer-term retention value — which matters especially for Win-Back and Churn Watchlist, where the strategic goal may be preventing future churn rather than immediate revenue.
